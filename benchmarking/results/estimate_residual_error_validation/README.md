# Validation of `estimate_residual_error` against known error rates

720 cells. Generated by `benchmarking/validate_estimate_residual_error.jl`, which
is committed and reproduces this table from a bare clone with no external tools:

```bash
julia --project=. benchmarking/validate_estimate_residual_error.jl
```

## What was measured, and why the ground truth is exact

Reads are produced by mutating a known reference at a known per-base rate. No
aligner and no read simulator is involved, so `true_error` is the parameter that
was *applied*, not a measurement of it. This deliberately avoids confounding
estimator bias with read-simulator model error.

Axes: true error {0.001, 0.01, 0.05, 0.10} x coverage {5, 10, 30, 50, 100} x
strand {single, double} x error mode {substitution, indel-heavy} x quality mode
{calibrated, overconfident, absent} x 3 seeds. Reference 20 kb, reads 5 kb,
`k_ref = 13`, `solid_min = 2` (the function's defaults).

The quality axis exists because `estimate_residual_error` returns
`max(kmer_component, quality_component)`. A perfectly calibrated Phred makes the
combined number look exact regardless of how the k-mer component behaves — which
is a property of the test, not of the estimator. `overconfident` reports 3x better
than truth, the direction real basecallers err in. `absent` removes quality
entirely, isolating the k-mer signal.

## Headline: on realistic input, the estimator IS its k-mer component

With an overconfident Phred, `max()` selects the k-mer estimate in every cell —
the `overconfident` and `absent` columns below are identical to six decimals.

Combined estimate at C=30, substitution, double-strand:

| true error | calibrated | overconfident | absent |
| ---------- | ---------- | ------------- | ------ |
| 0.001      | 0.0011     | 0.0011        | 0.0011 |
| 0.010      | 0.0100     | 0.0096        | 0.0096 |
| 0.050      | 0.0501     | 0.0434        | 0.0434 |
| 0.100      | 0.1000     | 0.0867        | 0.0867 |

This is a genuine design strength: taking the MORE CONSERVATIVE of the two
signals means an overconfident basecaller cannot drag the estimate down. But it
also means that on any real ONT data — where Phred is known to be miscalibrated,
and where medaka/DeepVariant/Clair3 all replace basecaller Q outright — the
estimator's behaviour is exactly the k-mer component's behaviour, biases included.

Across all 720 cells the k-mer component won `max()` 335 times, quality won 145,
and 240 had no quality at all.

## Finding 1: the k-mer component's bias REVERSES SIGN with coverage

Predicted before the run (over-estimation at low coverage, because a k-mer is
"solid" only at count >= 2 and even error-free k-mers occur once at low depth).
Confirmed — but the full picture has two competing mechanisms, and the second was
not predicted: at high coverage *erroneous* k-mers also start recurring and
become solid, pushing the estimate BELOW truth.

k-mer component, quality absent, substitution, double-strand:

| true error | C=5    | C=10   | C=30   | C=50   | C=100  |
| ---------- | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ |
| 0.001      | 0.0059 | 0.0023 | 0.0011 | 0.0010 | 0.0010 |
| 0.010      | 0.0179 | 0.0115 | 0.0096 | 0.0092 | 0.0084 |
| 0.050      | 0.0698 | 0.0525 | 0.0434 | 0.0391 | 0.0314 |
| 0.100      | 0.1424 | 0.1116 | 0.0867 | 0.0779 | 0.0639 |

Bias (estimate - truth):

| true error | C=5     | C=10    | C=30    | C=50    | C=100   |
| ---------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| 0.001      | +0.0049 | +0.0013 | +0.0001 | +0.0000 | +0.0000 |
| 0.010      | +0.0079 | +0.0015 | -0.0004 | -0.0008 | -0.0016 |
| 0.050      | +0.0198 | +0.0025 | -0.0066 | -0.0109 | -0.0186 |
| 0.100      | +0.0424 | +0.0116 | -0.0133 | -0.0221 | -0.0361 |

The zero crossing sits near C = 10-30 and the magnitude grows with true error. At
e = 0.10 and C = 100 the estimate is 0.0639 against a truth of 0.100 — a 36%
relative under-estimate. At C = 5 it is 0.1424, a 42% over-estimate.

## Finding 2: double-strand reads are biased high, in every matched pair

Predicted before the run. `_kmer_spectrum_residual_error` hashes with
`_dynamic_k_window_hash`, which is NOT canonical, while the same file defines
`_dynamic_k_canonical_window_hash` whose own comment warns that non-canonical
hashing counts a locus and its reverse complement as two different k-mers,
"HALVING the measured coverage on both-strand read sets".

Double minus single, quality absent, substitution:

| true error | C=5     | C=10    | C=30    | C=50    | C=100   |
| ---------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| 0.001      | +0.0028 | +0.0010 | +0.0001 | +0.0001 | +0.0000 |
| 0.010      | +0.0058 | +0.0016 | +0.0005 | +0.0007 | +0.0012 |
| 0.050      | +0.0171 | +0.0064 | +0.0057 | +0.0074 | +0.0088 |
| 0.100      | +0.0288 | +0.0178 | +0.0113 | +0.0129 | +0.0140 |

**Higher in 20 of 20 matched pairs.** The single/double arms are a true paired
contrast: both draw identical fragments and identical error draws, and only the
strand orientation differs. Note the bias does not vanish with coverage, which is
what the halved-effective-coverage mechanism predicts — it is multiplicative on
depth, not an additive small-sample artifact. The fix already exists in the same
file and is simply not called here.

## Finding 3: the docstring's indel claim is not supported

The docstring states that because an indel disrupts many downstream k-mers, the
estimator "(correctly) reports a higher effective error for indel-heavy reads."
At matched per-base error rate, that is not what happens. Indel-heavy minus
substitution ranges from **-0.0052 to +0.0024** and changes sign with coverage —
i.e. no material effect in either direction.

The likely mechanism: k-mers are position-independent, so a deletion does not
corrupt everything downstream. It destroys only the ~k-1 k-mers spanning the
junction, while a substitution destroys ~k. Under a matched budget, indel-bearing
reads are marginally *less* disruptive.

**Scope limit on this finding:** the model applies isolated single-base indels.
Real ONT indels cluster in homopolymers, which is not tested here and could
behave differently. The claim to take away is narrow: the docstring's assertion
is unsupported *as stated* and should not be relied on without a homopolymer test.

## Finding 4: the problem is bias, not noise — you cannot average it away

Replicate CV across 3 seeds, k-mer component, quality absent, substitution:

- e >= 0.01 at C >= 10: CV mostly below 0.02, max 0.044
- e = 0.001: CV rises to 0.34 at C = 10 and 0.25 at C = 5, where the signal is
  near the resolution floor

So the estimator is *precise* and *biased*. More replicates do not help.

## Consequence for the proposed k rule

The pre-registration proposes selecting k as `k >= 1/e - 1`. Feeding it this
estimator, with realistic (overconfident or absent) quality — these two columns
are identical, per the headline above:

| true error | k true | C=5 | C=10 | C=30 | C=50 | C=100 |
| ---------- | ------ | --- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ----- |
| 0.001      | 999    | 166 | 413  | 891  | 966  | 1007  |
| 0.010      | 99     | 53  | 87   | 103  | 107  | 117   |
| 0.050      | 19     | 13  | 18   | 22   | 24   | 30    |
| 0.100      | 9      | 6   | 7    | 10   | 11   | 14    |

Two failure directions, both material:

- **At low coverage, k is UNDER-selected** — 6 instead of 9 at e=0.10/C=5, and 166
  instead of 999 at e=0.001/C=5. This pushes k toward the small-k regime that is
  independently known to be degenerate. It is the same direction as, and a
  plausible contributor to, the low-coverage assembly collapse seen elsewhere in
  this project.
- **At high coverage, k is OVER-selected** — 30 instead of 19 at e=0.05/C=100.

The estimator is accurate enough for the rule only in a band around C = 10-30,
which is precisely where its two bias mechanisms cancel rather than where it is
sound.

## What this does NOT establish

- Nothing about **real reads**. Ground truth here is a parameter, not a
  measurement, and the error model is uniform-random substitutions and isolated
  indels.
- Nothing about **homopolymer-clustered indels**, the dominant real ONT mode.
- Nothing about **non-uniform coverage**. Coverage is uniform by construction;
  real data is not, and the solid/non-solid threshold is coverage-sensitive.
- Nothing about **repeat-rich or low-complexity genomes**. The reference is random
  sequence, which is the friendliest possible case for a k-mer spectrum method.

Each of these can only make the estimator behave worse than measured here.
